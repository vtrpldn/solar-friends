# Gameplay ideas for later

These are ideas to explore, not committed implementation scope. Keep planning meaningful and resident chores autonomous.

- **Community fridge:** shared leftovers, visitor produce and small social disagreements about food.
- **Gambiarra engineering:** improvised barrel showers, bicycle washing machines, pallet furniture and repurposed batteries, with maintenance tradeoffs.
- **Saturday mutirão:** invite neighbors to help with a big project; provide lunch, tools and space, then return the favor.
- **Escaped goats:** open gates, eaten seedlings and residents coaxing animals home; temperament and fences matter.
- **Village group chat:** observations tied to actual simulation conditions, with links to relevant actions.
- **Traveling hardware truck:** limited secondhand supplies, reliability tradeoffs and physically visible stock.
- **Outdoor movie nights:** a sheet, projector, seating, snacks, suitable weather and enough stored power.
- **Ground that records activity:** mud, wheel ruts, puddles and regrowth reflect use and drainage.
- **Swap corner:** exchange seedlings, furniture, tools and surplus produce with neighbors.
- **Individual garden plots:** residents cultivate staples, flowers or unusual crops in their spare time.
- **Beloved trees:** preserve a chosen tree and build shaded community spaces around it.
- **Workshop clutter:** borrowed tools and unfinished work make storage and tool carts useful.
- **Resident-made decorations:** pottery, signs, chimes, hammocks and mosaics emerge from hobbies; players choose placement.
- **Roadside honesty stall:** passing traffic buys produce and crafts; freshness, placement and physical restocking matter.

Capybaras are implemented as occasional ambient riverbank visitors. Crop damage and fence-aware wandering remain future extensions.

## Earning a living in the village

The repair shed and school visits are the first two ideas to explore. Repairs could bring in cash early using a workbench the residents already need. Hosting a class could give a settled village a reason to invest in the outdoor classroom, kitchen, toilets and rain shelter.

- **Village repair shed.** Neighbors could drop off a broken tool or wheelbarrow. Bento or Cauã would quote a price and pickup date, reserve parts and collect payment when the owner returns. If the village wheelbarrow breaks the day before a customer's repair is due, the player chooses who gets the next turn at the bench. [Equipment repair in Campinas](https://kimuramaquinas.com.br/)
- **School visits and workshops.** A school could book Nina for a field visit, or neighbors could sign up for basketry with Iara or farm cooking with Luz. Charge per place. The number of guests the village can host would depend on available residents, food, water, toilets and shelter. Sítio Vale das Cabras in Campinas already hosts environmental education visits for schools. [Sítio Vale das Cabras](https://sitiovaledascabras.com.br/)
- **Weekly food subscriptions.** Families could pay monthly for a weekly produce box at a shared pickup point. Players would decide how many households to take on, stagger planting and keep enough food for residents between harvests. A Campinas grower lists weekly boxes from R$231 per month. At that price, ten subscriptions would bring in R$2,310 a month before growing, packing and delivery costs. [Local produce subscription](https://sitiovaledascabras.com.br/)
- **Compost collection.** Households could pay for weekly food-scrap pickup. A driver could drop off produce boxes and collect scraps on the return leg. Vehicle space, clean buckets and composting capacity would limit the route. For comparison, Rede Composta lists R$80 a month for weekly pickup of a 15-liter bucket. [Rede Composta collection plans](https://www.redecomposta.com.br/coleta-organica/)
- **Weekend farm stays.** Guests could book a spare cabin and add breakfast or a guided walk. Before building a second house for rental income, the player would weigh expected bookings against residents' need for beds, cleaning time, water use and empty weekdays. [Rural lodging in Joaquim Egídio](https://pousadaquintadotorto.com.br/)

**School visit balance example.** Nina takes home R$172 from a full shift in town. Suppose a visit uses one resident-day including preparation and cleanup, tickets cost R$35 per student, supplies and snacks cost R$8 per student, and setup costs R$60 per group. Eight students would leave R$156 after those costs. Nine would leave R$183, enough to beat Nina's shift. A group of twelve would leave R$264. These are illustrative game prices.

**Possible independence milestone.** Cover food imports, fuel, utilities and upkeep for four consecutive weeks without town wages or drawing down savings. Both a low-cost village and a village with several busy businesses could reach it.
